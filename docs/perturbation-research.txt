BLCKBAUX PERTURBATION RESEARCH
2026-10-13. Mathematical research program and first implementation.

PURPOSE AND SCOPE

Discover full-parameter derivative-free algorithms together with proofs of their
properties. The objects are the actual model, objective construction, proposal
law, fitted estimator, selection rule, retained memory, and execution costs.
Lean is the semantic and proof environment. Rust/Metal/CUDA remain production
execution. No gradient oracle or latent parameter search is introduced.

The declared workload has 1,177,756,440 logical model parameters. Results below
are symbolic in the parameter count or even in an arbitrary real normed space.
A theorem over a large space is not a proposal to allocate dense covariance or
Hessian matrices of that size. Structured implicit operators and their costs
must be studied separately. Full-parameter coverage is an independent property;
an update's number of nonzero entries does not establish reachable-space rank.

The 200 ms transaction target and 1000 ms ceiling remain machine/workload
targets. This work does not establish either target or superiority to Muon.

NEW MATHEMATICAL IMPLEMENTATION

CURRENT PROPOSAL-POLICY SOURCES

The immediate synthesis target is the history-conditioned proposal law, not a
fixed noise distribution. These sources contribute distinct reusable ideas:

  Bubeck, Munos, Stoltz, and Szepesvari, X-Armed Bandits, JMLR 2011.
  https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v12/bubeck11a.html
  Near-optimality geometry can control regret without identifying the ambient
  parameter count with the relevant search dimension.

  Kpotufe, k-NN Regression Adapts to Local Intrinsic Dimension, 2011.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.4300
  Local ball-mass growth supports query-specific neighbor counts and error rates.

  Malherbe and Vayatis, Global Optimization of Lipschitz Functions, ICML 2017.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/malherbe17a.html
  Lipschitz regularity alone retains a covering-number curse; a useful theorem
  must state the additional local geometry exploited by the proposal law.

  Rashidi, Johnstonbaugh, and Gao, Cylindrical Thompson Sampling, AISTATS 2024.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/rashidi24a.html
  Candidate-sampling geometry is a modular BO component with material effects
  in high dimension. Its cylindrical construction is evidence, not yet an BLCKBAUX
  proposal or a transferred theorem.

  Park et al., Elucidating Subspace Perturbation in Zeroth-Order Optimization,
  2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19099
  Structured perturbation quality depends on alignment rather than sparsity or
  rank alone. BLCKBAUX will not import its gradient-estimation interface as a premise.

  Tan et al., Perturbation-efficient Zeroth-order Optimization, 2025.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20314
  Proposal generation cost and random-number traffic are algorithmic constraints,
  which matches the BLCKBAUX requirement that a proposal theorem carry machine cost.

  Fan and Pleiss, Adaptive Candidate Point Thompson Sampling, AISTATS 2026.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/fan26c.html
  Fixed candidate discretizations become sparse in high dimension. Their use of
  surrogate gradients is not adopted; the relevant question is how an adaptive
  executable candidate law increases mass near improving parameter transitions.

  Papenmeier, Poloczek, and Nardi, Understanding High-Dimensional Bayesian
  Optimization, ICML 2025.
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/papenmeier25a.html
  Their empirical diagnosis favors local-search behavior and careful surrogate
  length-scale fitting. This motivates auditing our radius and LOOCV machinery;
  it does not transfer a GP result to the ENN estimator.

  Roberts and Royer, Direct Search Based on Probabilistic Descent in Reduced
  Spaces, SIAM Journal on Optimization 2023.
  https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1488569
  The reusable proof interface is a probability that the generated polling set
  contains a useful direction. BLCKBAUX replaces their direction condition by the
  derivative-free event that an executable candidate enters a neighborhood of
  a better state. Their random-subspace complexity theorem is not imported.

  Jin, Cen, and Tan, Steering the Noise: Turning Random Perturbations into
  Effective Descent for Memory-Efficient LLM Fine-Tuning, 2026.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04710
  History- or prior-guided perturbation alignment is relevant comparative
  evidence. Its gradient-estimation target is not an BLCKBAUX premise; the question
  here is whether history increases objective-neighborhood capture per unit work.

Optimization/ObjectiveSearch.lean now defines BallMass only on an explicit
radius interval. This avoids pretending that a finite executable candidate law
has continuous power-law mass at arbitrarily small scales. scaledBound proves
that a coefficient-times-radius^exponent lower mass bound yields an explicit
expected progress lower bound. linearBound chooses radius gain/(2*L), retaining
half the reachable gain without using a derivative. zeroMass records the hard
support boundary: no generated candidate in a ball means exactly zero capture.

selectBound is the operational bridge for the actual score-and-select pool. If
one executable member is within radius r of a state with gain g, the selected
member has certified progress at least

  g - modulus(r) - 2 * simultaneous_score_error - selection_slack.

This statement does not sample a slot uniformly and does not assume candidate
independence. uniformLaw, hitMass, quarterMass, and slotBound are a separate
uniform-choice baseline: a captured member of a four-slot uniform law carries
mass 1/4. They must not be reported as the deployed acquisition law.

PoolHit and seedBound put the probability in the correct place. A random seed
generates a complete dependent pool; the event is that at least one member hits
the neighborhood. If rejection protects every miss round from objective
increase, seedBound proves

  expected selected progress
    >= pool-hit probability
       * (g - modulus(r) - 2*score_error - selection_slack).

This theorem is pointwise in the current history. A history-conditioned policy
is obtained by instantiating the seed law and generator at each history; the
remaining research obligation is a uniform or trajectory-valid hit bound.

SOURCE POOL AUDIT

The active operator-BO TOMLs specify four arms with one candidate per arm.
Metal domain-separates each arm root and applies one reliability radius to all
four candidates. The former `CurrentPool.lean` model instead encoded the legacy
one-arm geometry as two streams crossed with two radii. It was deleted because
its rank and covering conclusions were not statements about the active source.

The replacement obligation is layout-parametric. For a resolved arm/slot
configuration, prove that the host and each backend implement the same seed and
radius map, then derive the number of distinct directions after finite-precision
materialization. Candidate count alone does not establish independence,
coverage, useful alignment, or a regret rate.

The next proof obligation is therefore precise. For a history H and incumbent
x, define the source-generated random four-candidate set C(H,x). Prove or refute
a lower bound on

  probability(exists c in C(H,x), distance(c, y) <= r)

for every declared reachable comparison state y in the objective class, with
the generator's hash dependence, radius pairing, finite precision, and work
account included. The selectBound theorem then converts that event into actual
selected progress whenever the ENN score envelope is valid.

Two-point gradient-estimation algorithms and Markov-chain candidate transitions
are not the current interface. They remain comparative evidence only.

Optimization/Smooth.lean
  Uses Mathlib.Analysis.Calculus.TaylorIntegral's multidimensional Taylor formula,
  not only the scalar-input Taylor module. The exact expansion is
    F(theta + delta) = F(theta) + DF(theta)[delta] + R(theta, delta),
    R = integral_0^1 (1-t) D^2 F(theta+t delta)[delta,delta] dt.
  C2 is required locally along each segment, not globally on parameter space.
  A directional second-derivative upper cap C implies R <= C/2. Two mirrored
  segments then give best change <= -abs(DF(theta)[delta]) + C/2.
  The curvature penalty does not cancel. Derivatives are analysis objects;
  the executable optimizer does not receive or estimate them through this result.

Optimization/Moments.lean
  Uses Mathlib probability measures, MemLp, covariance, matrix trace, and its
  multivariateGaussian construction. For square-integrable parameter entries,
    E[delta^T B delta] = tr(B Cov(delta)) + mean(delta)^T B mean(delta).
  B need not be positive or symmetric for the algebraic identity; using it as a
  curvature upper bound is a separate obligation. Isotropic zero-mean proposals
  specialize to variance * trace(B). Gaussian mean and covariance come from
  library constructions, not user-supplied moment assertions.
  This is not proof that the current indexed/sign/frozen-basis generator samples
  a multivariate Gaussian, has isotropic covariance, or provides useful alignment.

Optimization/Search.lean
  An exploratory three-candidate family contains the incumbent and mirrored
  full-parameter proposals. Executed candidate states are distinct from ideal
  analytical states. Existing UniformError and ApproxMin compose with Taylor:
    selected executed change <= -abs(DF(theta)[delta]) + C/2
                                + 2*score_error + selection_slack
                                + execution_penalty.
  Execution fidelity is a directional objective bound on comparison candidates;
  a norm error is not silently substituted for an objective error.
  expected integrates a pointwise progress bound and replaces its quadratic
  expectation by the covariance/mean trace formula. Loss, alignment, penalty,
  and second-moment integrability are required explicitly. It may be used at a
  fixed history with that history's conditional law; it is not a complete
  continuous-law trajectory formalization.
  The three-candidate research mechanism is not the deployed four-slot pool,
  and this theorem does not claim that UCB scores satisfy UniformError.

Optimization/Rate.lean
  For existing Synthesis policies with arbitrary state and memory types and
  finite conditional feedback branches, geometric/alignment dominance implies
  a local simple-regret contraction. Finite expectation recursion derives
    expected terminal regret <= rho^T * initial regret
                                + eta * sum_(i<T) rho^i.
  For 0 <= rho < 1, its envelope is
    rho^T * (initial regret - eta/(1-rho)) + eta/(1-rho).
  Mathlib proves that envelope tends to eta/(1-rho). A fixed error does not
  vanish because a controller contracts. The theorem proves the envelope's
  limit, not convergence of the actual policy to that floor or equality to it.
  The local dominance property must be established from proposal geometry,
  objective structure, observation law, estimator, and selector. No such
  production BLCKBAUX contraction is asserted. Factor/penalty are symbolic,
  not measured constants or desired rates inserted as established facts.
  vanishes proves that the policy's actual expected regret tends to zero when
  the additive penalty is zero, contraction is proved, and the comparator is
  globally optimal. These premises are not established for the source optimizer.

Forward/Sensitivity.lean
  updateSplit expands the actual real-arithmetic Mixing.update difference into
  input-state change, residual-coefficient change, branch change, and output-
  coefficient change. The operator view borrows Mathlib continuous linear maps:
    ||A(y)y+b(y)-A(x)x-b(x)||
      <= ||A(y)|| ||y-x|| + ||A(y)-A(x)|| ||x|| + ||b(y)-b(x)||.
  With separately justified caps, the local sensitivity bound is
    transport_cap + predictor_Lipschitz * state_radius + branch_Lipschitz.
  Thus a unit transport norm does not prove full learned-block contraction.
  The predictor/branch constants, flat parameter indexing, and floating-point
  execution correspondence are still open.

RESEARCH OBJECTS TO SYNTHESIZE

1. Proposal geometry and exploration
   Study dependent signs, mirrored proposals, anisotropic full-support laws,
   block covariance, mixtures of exploratory and learned proposals, symmetry-
   preserving operators combined with unrestricted updates, and radius schedules.
   Obligations: directional alignment, second moments, reachable support,
   generator/distribution correspondence, quantized executed support, sampling
   work, and parameter traffic. Orthogonal-only weight transformations preserve
   singular values and cannot alone provide unrestricted parameter optimization.
   Possible findings: useful alignment/cost tradeoffs, constructive proposal
   families, and counterexamples to isotropy or energy as sufficient criteria.

2. Forward-program sensitivity and nonsmooth boundaries
   Study the encoder, RMS/SiLU, attention probabilities, PISA support changes,
   expert routing, finite Sinkhorn normalization, learned mHC, recurrent layer
   visits, and hierarchical decoder jointly. Stable routing regions permit
   local calculus; boundary crossings need explicit jump/probability estimates.
   Establish actual all-pairs routing margins, not just a two-score inequality.
   Compare componentwise product bounds with structure-aware composition bounds.
   Possible findings: tighter objective curvature, cancellation justified by
   actual structure, and mechanisms that reduce expensive objective evaluation.

3. Estimation, adaptive neighbors, and uncertainty
   Study local metric geometry, adaptive neighbor counts, sampled distance error,
   LOOCV fitting under adaptive data, correlated patches, shared-world paired
   evaluation, predictor uncertainty versus mean uncertainty, and baseline drift.
   Objects include the existing Neighbor/Response/Bayes modules. A barycenter is
   not automatically a Bayesian posterior; coherent prior/observation laws and
   calibrated simultaneous errors must be identified or replaced by other
   explicitly justified uncertainty constructions.
   Possible findings: local-dimension error rates, improved weighting, valid
   uncertainty combinations, or impossibility under the available observations.

4. Memory and information
   Distinguish total queries, retained observations, retained sufficient
   statistics, parameter count, local metric dimension, and curvature spectrum.
   Investigate bounded-history forgetting, resets, moving incumbents, streaming
   summaries, and how information loss affects candidate selection.
   Possible findings: sufficient retained state for a specified objective class,
   lower bounds on memory/query requirements, and bounded-memory policies whose
   regret certificates account for discarded information. No universal D or D^2
   observation count is known for this construction.

5. Objective construction and code-generation outcomes
   Distinguish fixed-corpus NLL, population NLL, code-execution success, held-out
   task failure, runtime/memory constraints, and vector objectives/Pareto order.
   Study paired objective noise, evaluation batching, drift, generalization, and
   the circumstances under which reducing an objective controls coding outcomes.
   The checked loss-only teacher decoder equivalence applies to target NLL in
   the forward view, not arbitrary generation rewards. Every comparison with
   Muon must share objective, evaluation protocol, start, and resource account.

6. Algorithm/proof search and resources
   A fixed objective/noise class and resource constraints define a search task.
   Candidate proposal, inference, selection, and memory rules must compile
   separately from noncomputable analysis. Proofs cannot weaken the task after
   seeing a candidate. Local potential or progress certificates should connect
   to terminal simple regret and declared work; finite-horizon and asymptotic
   statements must remain distinct.
   Account for scored versus materialized versus evaluated candidates, batch
   width versus sequential depth, forward passes, launches, synchronization,
   traffic, host work, sorting, and random-generator retries. Kernel fusion can
   change these costs without establishing a statistical improvement. A complete
   machine envelope is required to turn work into a sufficient latency bound.

PRIMARY RESEARCH CONNECTIONS AND TRANSFER OBLIGATIONS

Stochastic Three Points (Bergou/Gorbunov/Richtarik):
  https://arxiv.org/html/1902.03591v3
  Direct selection without a gradient estimator for fixed/decreasing step rules.
  Directional-law constants and smoothness control progress. Nonconvex
  stationarity is not global simple regret. Adaptive law changes must retain
  the stated alignment/moment properties. Not itself a Bayesian optimizer.

k-NN Regression Adapts to Local Intrinsic Dimension (Kpotufe):
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.4300
  Local geometry and adaptive neighbor count are relevant. Its sampling,
  regularity, noise, and metric hypotheses must be restated. Adaptive optimizer
  observations, sampled distances, and bounded-history forgetting need new work.

GP-UCB (Srinivas/Krause/Kakade/Seeger):
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.3995
  Information gain and kernel spectra connect uncertainty to cumulative regret
  under stated GP/RKHS hypotheses. Transfer to terminal recommendation regret
  and the implemented ENN predictor is not supplied by importing the paper.

Sufficient Statistics and the Burkholder Method (Foster/Rakhlin/Sridharan):
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v75/foster18b.html
  Algorithm construction from regret certificates and retained statistics in
  its online-learning setting. A black-box pretraining/simple-regret version
  would require an appropriate game, feedback model, and certificate.

Derivative-free stochastic convex lower bounds (Shamir):
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v30/Shamir13.html
  Dimension/query limitations are class- and oracle-dependent. They cannot be
  dismissed by proof search, nor applied to our model without checking scope.

FORMALIZATION LIMITS AND ACCEPTANCE

Mathlib supplies multivariate Taylor integral formulas, Gaussian measures,
covariance, convexity/Birkhoff constructions, spectral foundations, martingales,
and asymptotic filters. It does not supply a ready-made ENN regret theorem,
complete Sinkhorn/routing sensitivity, or verified Metal/CUDA execution.
First-order model theory is a separate mathematical view; these analytical
probability and operator models do not become model-theory results by naming.

Run ./blckbaux research check for the complete library and repository naming lint;
./blckbaux research status reports checked findings and open source obligations.
Audit theorem dependencies: no sorry, new axioms, or unproved numerical caps
may establish a research result. Standard classical Mathlib axioms are distinct
from assuming the desired theorem. No GPU experiment is launched by this work.
